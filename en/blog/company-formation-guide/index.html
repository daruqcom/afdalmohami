Guide to Setting Up a Company in Saudi Arabia

This guide explains Guide to Setting Up a Company in Saudi Arabia in plain language, with the practical steps anyone facing this situation in Saudi Arabia needs to know.

This guide presents the steps for company formation in Saudi Arabia, from choosing the appropriate legal form to obtaining the commercial registration, in practical language suited to new entrepreneurs.

Forming a company in Saudi Arabia starts with a fundamental decision: which legal form suits your project? The main options include a sole establishment for small single-owner projects, a limited liability company most common for protecting partners' personal assets, and a joint stock company for major projects planning to offer shares or attract multiple investors.

Choosing the Appropriate Legal Form

A sole establishment doesn't separate your personal and business assets, meaning business activity obligations may extend to your personal assets. A limited liability company protects these assets, and suits most small and medium projects with one or more partners. A joint stock company has more complex requirements but is necessary if you plan major expansion or a future public offering.

Formation Steps Through the Istethmer Platform

Procedures start with reserving the trade name and confirming it doesn't conflict with previously registered names, then drafting the incorporation contract (for companies) or activity declaration (for sole establishments). These documents are submitted through the Ministry of Commerce's electronic platform, and the commercial registration is issued within a few days if documents are complete and free of conflicts.

Foreign Investor Requirements

If one of the partners or investors is foreign, an additional route through the Ministry of Investment (MISA) is added to obtain a foreign investment licence before or concurrent with company registration. The required licence type varies by activity: services, industrial, commercial, or others, each with different conditions and capital requirements.

Additional Sector Licences

Some activities need additional special licences after the commercial registration: such as food activities needing a Saudi Food and Drug Authority licence, or health activities needing Ministry of Health approval, or educational activities subject to the Ministry of Education. Ignoring these sector licences may mean operating a violating activity even with a valid commercial registration.

The Importance of a Written Partnership Agreement

Even in the simplest partnerships, we always recommend documenting a written partnership agreement defining ownership percentages, management authorities, dispute resolution mechanisms, and terms for any partner's future entry or exit. The absence of this documentation is the most common cause of partner disputes that later reach the courts.

After Formation: Ongoing Obligations

Legal work doesn't end with the commercial registration's issuance; the company needs to open a commercial bank account, register for zakat and tax, register for social insurance (GOSI) if it has employees, and follow up on annual commercial registration renewal.

When You Need a Lawyer at the Formation Stage

Although basic procedures have become electronic and relatively simple, legal review of the incorporation contract and partnership agreement remains necessary to avoid gaps that only surface at the first disagreement between partners or dispute with a third party.

Documents Needed to Start the Formation Process

Starting needs: identity of all founding partners, a clear description of the target commercial activity, and a proposed trade name (with backup alternatives). No additional documents are needed at the initial consultation stage.

Common Question: How Long Does the Complete Formation Process Take?

Simple cases with a clear legal form and complete documents may be completed within a few days, while cases involving foreign investors or additional sector licences need longer, potentially reaching weeks.

Conclusion: A Sound Legal Foundation Protects Your Venture

Forming a company on a sound legal foundation from the start, from choosing the correct form to documenting the partnership agreement, saves you costly disputes later. Invest time in legal review before signing, not after the first disagreement arises.

Practical Case: Two Partners Disagree After a Year of Formation

Imagine two partners who formed a limited liability company without a written partnership agreement detailing roles, then disagreed over who manages daily operations and who approves major financial decisions. Without a clear document, this disagreement becomes a complex dispute needing court intervention to interpret the basic incorporation contract, while it could have been entirely avoided with a clear partnership agreement from day one.

Converting a Sole Establishment to a Limited Liability Company

Many entrepreneurs start with a sole establishment then need to convert to a limited liability company as the business grows or a new partner joins. This conversion is a defined statutory procedure preserving the commercial registration and business history of the activity, and doesn't need liquidating the old establishment and forming a new entity from scratch.

Capital Requirements by Activity Type

Minimum capital requirements vary by activity type and legal form; some activities like foreign investment in certain sectors require a higher minimum, while no specific minimum is required for ordinary limited liability companies in most simple commercial activities.

Choosing the Trade Name: Important Legal Considerations

The proposed trade name must not conflict with previously registered names, must not violate public decency, and must not contain names of countries or international organizations without permission. We always recommend preparing 3-4 backup names when applying to avoid the first name's rejection and having to restart the reservation procedure.

Special Requirements for E-Commerce Activities

Business activities conducted entirely online sometimes need an additional licence from the E-Commerce Authority alongside the ordinary commercial registration, especially if the activity includes direct sales to consumers through a platform specific to the company.

Getting these foundational details right saves significant time and cost as your business grows.
