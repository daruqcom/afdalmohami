Medical Malpractice Compensation in Saudi Arabia

This guide explains Medical Malpractice Compensation in Saudi Arabia in plain language, with the practical steps anyone facing this situation in Saudi Arabia needs to know.

This guide explains how to prove medical error and claim compensation in Saudi Arabia, from filing a complaint with the competent technical committee to the final compensation assessment.

Proving medical error under Saudi law doesn't rely on the patient's opinion or even another doctor's individual opinion, but on a technical report issued by a specialized committee examining the complete medical record and determining whether there was an actual deviation from accepted medical standards and practices, and whether this deviation is the cause of the harm suffered by the patient.

Steps to File a Medical Malpractice Complaint

The process begins by filing a formal complaint with the medical error adjudication committee, attaching the complete medical record of the case and any relevant reports or medical images. The complaint must be filed within the statutory deadline from the date of discovering the harm or incident, and delay beyond this deadline may forfeit the right to claim.

How the Technical Committee Evaluates the Error

The committee includes physicians specialized in the relevant field, and they review the medical record in detail: the diagnosis, treatment decisions made, and their consistency with globally and locally accepted medical protocols. The committee issues its report specifying: did an actual medical error occur? And is this error the direct cause of harm, or is the harm a result of a known medical complication despite adherence to scientific standards?

The Difference Between Medical Error and a Known Complication

This distinction is fundamental: not every negative treatment outcome is medical malpractice. A technically successful surgery may carry a known, rare complication without this meaning the doctor was negligent. Medical error means an actual deviation from the standard expected of a careful doctor in the same specialty and circumstances, such as a clear misdiagnosis, wrong medication dosage, or forgetting a surgical instrument inside the patient's body.

Compensation Elements Due

After the error is established, the file moves to the compensation assessment stage, which includes: current and future corrective treatment costs, lost earnings if the error affected work capacity, permanent disability if any at its medically estimated percentage, and moral damage for the pain and psychological suffering the patient and family go through in serious cases.

The Role of Independent Medical Experts

In complex cases, independent medical experts can be engaged to more precisely assess future damages, especially when the error results in long-term disability needing precise estimation of future care and treatment costs over years.

When You Need a Specialized Lawyer for These Cases

Given the technical complexity of these cases, specialized legal representation is necessary to build a strong file before the technical committee, and ensure a fair compensation assessment reflecting the actual extent of harm, not a token amount.

Documents Needed to File a Complaint

Filing the complaint needs: the complete medical record of the case, any relevant reports or radiology images, and a recent medical report documenting current harm if obtainable. No additional documents are needed at the initial consultation stage.

Common Question: Can a Complaint Be Filed Against a Doctor Who Left the Facility?

Yes, liability relates to the incident and the doctor who committed it at the time it occurred, regardless of their current workplace, and the healthcare facility also remains liable for oversight of its practices at the time of the incident.

Conclusion: Early Medical Documentation Protects Your Right

Whether you're a patient suspecting medical error or a healthcare facility needing to understand its legal position, precise, early medical documentation is the foundation any subsequent case before the competent technical committee is built on.

Practical Case: Delayed Diagnosis of a Serious Illness

Imagine a patient who visited a doctor with certain symptoms, and correct diagnosis was delayed for months despite available tests sufficient to detect the illness early, leading to condition deterioration. Here the technical committee examines: were the available tests sufficient for a careful doctor to detect the illness earlier? If yes, this is diagnostic delay constituting medical error deserving compensation covering the difference between the patient's condition had they been diagnosed early and their actual condition after the delay.

Facility Liability vs. Individual Doctor Liability

In some cases, the error is institutional, not individual, such as a shortage of qualified medical staff during an emergency, or a malfunctioning medical device not regularly maintained. In these cases, the healthcare facility itself bears liability alongside or instead of the direct doctor, and this institutional aspect is assessed within the technical committee's investigation.

Can Settlement Happen Outside the Technical Committee?

Yes, the healthcare facility and patient can reach an amicable settlement without waiting for the committee's final decision, especially if the facility recognizes clear negligence and wants to avoid lengthy procedures. This settlement must be legally documented in a form preserving the patient's full rights.

The Importance of Modern Digital Medical Records

With most healthcare facilities transitioning to electronic medical record systems, documenting treatment decisions and timing has become more precise, easing the technical committee's work reviewing the case. We always recommend patients request a complete copy of their digital medical record immediately upon suspecting any error, as the digital version is harder to later alter than old paper records.

Medical Insurance and Its Role in Covering Compensation

Many healthcare facilities and doctors carry professional liability insurance covering compensation amounts ruled in medical malpractice cases. This insurance eases the actual collection process for the affected patient, as it ensures a financially capable party exists to pay alongside the doctor or facility itself.
