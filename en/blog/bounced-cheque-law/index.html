Bounced Cheque Law & Penalties in Saudi Arabia

This guide explains Bounced Cheque Law & Penalties in Saudi Arabia in plain language, with the practical steps anyone facing this situation in Saudi Arabia needs to know.

This guide explains the bounced cheque law in Saudi Arabia and its penalties, with practical steps for collecting a returned cheque's value through either the criminal route or direct enforcement.

A cheque under Saudi law is a direct executive instrument, meaning a bounced cheque holder has two parallel routes to protect their right: direct enforcement before the enforcement court to collect the amount, and the criminal route if the elements of the offense of issuing a cheque without sufficient funds are present.

What Constitutes a Bounced Cheque?

A cheque bounces when presented to the bank for payment and rejected due to insufficient funds in the drawer's account, account closure, or any reason preventing actual payment. The bank then issues an official rejection notice (non-payment certificate) that becomes the primary document for any subsequent action.

The Direct Enforcement Route for Collecting the Cheque Value

Since the cheque is an executive instrument, its holder can go directly to the enforcement court without needing to file an ordinary lawsuit first. The enforcement request is submitted through the Najiz platform attaching the original cheque and the bank's non-payment certificate. If the drawer doesn't respond with payment within the specified period, compulsory enforcement measures are activated: bank account seizure, travel bans, and listing the non-complying party in the credit classification system.

The Criminal Penalty for Issuing a Bounced Cheque

Alongside the civil route, the law criminalizes issuing a cheque with knowledge of insufficient funds at the time of issuance or at the maturity date. The criminal penalty reaches imprisonment and a fine, escalating with repeated violation by the same drawer or multiple bounced cheques issued by them.

The Difference Between a Cheque as Security and a Cheque as Payment

Among the thorny issues in these cases: cheques issued as security for an obligation rather than as direct payment, such as security cheques for lease or contracting agreements. If the cheque was originally security not meant to be cashed except upon breach of the obligation, this may constitute a defense against enforcement or criminal prosecution, and needs precise proof through documents and correspondence accompanying the cheque's issuance.

When to Resort to the Criminal Route Instead of Direct Enforcement

The criminal route is a powerful pressure tool distinct from civil enforcement, especially if the drawer is evading enforcement procedures or hiding their assets. Filing a criminal report alongside the enforcement request increases pressure on the drawer for quick payment to avoid criminal accountability.

What If the Drawer Is a Company, Not an Individual?

When the cheque is issued by a company, criminal liability usually extends to the natural person who signed the cheque on the company's behalf, while civil liability remains with the company itself and its assets.

When You Need a Specialized Lawyer for Cheque Collection

Although the enforcement route seems formally simple, the drawer's potential defenses (like claiming the cheque was security) may complicate the file, and legal representation helps you choose the most suitable route and speed up actual collection of your right.

Documents Needed for the Enforcement Request

Filing the enforcement request needs: the original bounced cheque, the bank's non-payment certificate, and the cheque holder's identity. No additional documents are needed at the initial consultation stage.

Common Question: Can a Post-Dated Cheque Be Collected?

A cheque is payable on demand once presented for payment regardless of the date written on it, but it's advisable to confirm the agreed maturity date has actually arrived before presenting it to the bank to avoid any procedural issue.

Conclusion: The Cheque Is a Powerful Tool for Collecting Your Right

The cheque's direct executive power under Saudi law gives you an effective tool for quickly collecting your right, especially when procedures are managed correctly from the start. Don't delay taking action once the cheque is rejected; quick action preserves your right and pressures the drawer to pay.

Practical Case: A Security Cheque for a Lease Agreement

Imagine a property owner who requested security cheques from a tenant to be cashed only upon the tenant's contract breach (like non-payment of rent). If the owner cashes one of these cheques without an actual breach by the tenant, the tenant can argue the cheque was conditional security, not a direct payment method, and needs to prove this through the original contract explicitly stating these cheques' nature as security.

Multiple Bounced Cheques from the Same Drawer

If a person issues several bounced cheques to different parties, the criminal penalty intensifies with each additional incident, and they may be classified as an organized fraudster if a repeated, deliberate pattern is established. Multiple cheque holders can coordinate among themselves to strengthen the criminal prosecution's position against the same drawer.

Electronic Cheques and Modern Proof Challenges

With the spread of electronic banking systems, some transactions occur through direct transfers instead of traditional paper cheques. If a similar bank transfer fails, the proof and enforcement mechanism differs from a traditional cheque, needing a different legal route depending on the type of banking document used.

The Difference Between Individual and Company Cheques in Prosecution

When the bounced cheque is issued from a company account, the investigation sometimes extends to determining who had actual signing authority at the time the cheque was issued, especially in companies with joint signature requirements. This detail may affect who bears personal criminal liability alongside the company's civil liability.
