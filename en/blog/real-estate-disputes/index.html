Real Estate & Rental Disputes in Saudi Arabia

This guide explains Real Estate & Rental Disputes in Saudi Arabia in plain language, with the practical steps anyone facing this situation in Saudi Arabia needs to know.

This guide explains the most common types of real estate disputes and rental disputes in Saudi Arabia, with practical steps for handling each type.

Real estate disputes in Saudi Arabia vary between ownership disputes, rental disputes between landlords and tenants, and real estate development disputes with developers. Each type has a different legal route and competent jurisdiction, and understanding this diversity helps you choose the correct route from the start.

Rental Disputes Through the Ejar Platform

With mandatory registration of residential and commercial lease contracts through the Ejar platform, registered contracts now have direct executive power in specific cases like delayed rent payment. A property owner can request eviction of a tenant delayed in payment through a relatively fast route if the contract is properly registered, while unregistered contracts need a slower ordinary judicial route.

Rent Increase Disputes Upon Renewal

Among recurring disputes: a tenant refusing a rent increase landlords see as justified, or vice versa. Resolving this dispute depends on the original contract's terms; if the contract specifies a clear mechanism for increase upon renewal, this mechanism applies; if the contract is silent, the matter is subject to negotiation or judicial assessment when disputed.

Rented Property Maintenance Disputes

The contract usually determines who bears maintenance responsibility: structural and basic maintenance usually falls to landlords, while daily operational maintenance falls to tenants. The absence of clear text in the contract opens the door to disputes over who bears the cost of a specific repair, which is why detailing this clause in the contract from the start is always recommended.

Property Ownership Disputes Among Heirs or Partners

When a property is owned by multiple parties (heirs or investment partners), disputes arise over division, sale, or management. The available legal solution is a judicial division case obligating all owners to sell or physically divide if the property can actually be separated without harming its value.

Delayed Delivery Disputes in Real Estate Projects

In off-plan sales projects registered within the Wafi program, the escrow account protects buyers from losing their money, and a buyer affected by significant delivery delay can claim compensation or even rescind the contract and recover paid amounts according to the contract terms and program regulations.

Choosing the Correct Route for Your Real Estate Dispute

Simple disputes like payment delay or minor maintenance are often resolved through Ejar platform mechanisms or direct negotiation, while major ownership disputes or serious delivery delay need full legal representation before the competent court.

When You Need a Specialized Real Estate Lawyer

The greater the property's financial value or the more complex the dispute's parties, the more necessary specialized legal representation becomes to protect your investment from risks that may not be obvious without specialized real estate legal expertise.

Documents Needed by Dispute Type

In rental disputes, having the registered lease contract and payment demand correspondence helps. In ownership disputes, having the title deed and partner or heir data helps. No additional documents are needed at the initial consultation stage.

Common Question: Is an Unregistered Lease Contract Void?

No, the contract remains valid between the parties, but it loses some fast-track enforcement benefits available to registered contracts, and any dispute over it needs a slower ordinary judicial route.

Conclusion: Understand Your Rights Before Signing or Escalating

Whether you're an owner, tenant, or real estate investor, understanding the legal framework governing your real estate relationship protects you from costly surprises. Documentation through the Ejar platform and prior legal review of any major contract are two essential, indispensable steps.

Practical Case: A Tenant Refuses Eviction Despite an Expired Contract

Imagine a lease contract whose term expired without renewal, and the tenant refuses eviction despite repeated notices from the owner. If the contract is registered through the Ejar platform, the owner can submit a direct eviction request based on the contractual term's expiry without needing to prove an additional reason, speeding up the procedure compared to unregistered contracts needing an ordinary judicial eviction case.

Down Payment Disputes in Real Estate Sale Deals

When a buyer pays a down payment to reserve a property then withdraws from the purchase, or the seller withdraws after receiving the down payment, the down payment agreement's terms (not the final sale contract) determine this amount's fate: is it forfeited to the seller if the buyer withdraws? Is it doubled as compensation if the seller withdraws? The answer depends entirely on the specific wording in the signed down payment agreement.

Real Estate Broker Liability for Misleading Information

If a real estate broker provided inaccurate information about a property (such as hiding a known defect or exaggerating actual area) leading to a wrong purchase decision, the broker can be held accountable for this negligence alongside or instead of the seller themselves, especially if the broker is officially licensed and subject to professional regulations governing disclosure duty.

Verifying the Property Record Before Any Transaction

We always recommend reviewing the official real estate registry to confirm the property is free of any mortgage, seizure, or existing dispute before purchase or long-term lease. This simple check, taking a few minutes through official platforms, prevents many costly surprises that may appear after completing the deal.

Parking and Shared Facility Disputes

In multi-unit residential and commercial complexes, disputes sometimes arise over using parking spaces or shared facilities like elevators and swimming pools. The co-owners' bylaws or building management system determine these rights, and any dispute over them first needs referring to this regulatory document.
