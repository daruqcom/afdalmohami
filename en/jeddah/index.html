We Cover All of Jeddah

Our team serves every district and area of Jeddah without exception, with the same response speed and service standard.

Our team in Jeddah provides legal advice and representation to individual and corporate clients in Saudi Arabia's main commercial port city and gateway for Hajj and Umrah pilgrims, with practical knowledge of its booming trade and real estate sectors.

Jeddah has a dual character: it's the largest commercial port on the Red Sea and Saudi Arabia's main gateway for millions of pilgrims heading to Mecca and Medina for Hajj and Umrah year-round. That geographic and economic position makes it one of the more legally active cities in the Kingdom — both in import/export activity and in a real estate market that's grown notably in recent years with coastal development projects.

Most requested services in Jeddah

Given the city's real estate boom, real estate and property law and real estate developer disputes top our Jeddah consultation list, especially with the number of housing projects and coastal complexes underway. We also handle a fair number of real estate brokerage disputes stemming from the multiple brokers active in the market. On the commercial side, company formation remains a recurring request from traders connected to import activity through Jeddah Islamic Port.

We also support clients operating in the hospitality and tourism sector, given its direct link to the Hajj and Umrah seasons, alongside handling traffic accident compensation cases, which run relatively high in a city with Jeddah's traffic volume.

Courts and competent authorities

Cases in Jeddah are distributed across the Commercial Court, the General Court for personal status and civil matters, and the Labor Court, all tracked electronically via the Najiz platform. For real estate disputes specifically, we also coordinate with the relevant municipal authorities when documentation or administrative settlement is needed.

Examples of cases we handle in Jeddah

Recurring scenarios include a client who bought a residential unit in a project that wasn't delivered on the agreed date and needs to claim against the developer, a trader who needs an import contract drafted to protect against customs-delay risk, or a family needing to settle a lease dispute after a contract ends. In every case, we start with a quick consultation to determine the best path before escalating to any official body.

How working with us in Jeddah works

As in the rest of our network, it starts on WhatsApp, with a quick assessment of your file by a specializing lawyer, then a clear, time-bound plan before any formal action. We keep you informed on your file as it develops — particularly important in real estate cases that may require coordinating several parties at once.

Initial consultation vs. full representation in Jeddah

In many real estate disputes, an initial advisory review is enough to assess how strong a claim is before escalating to any official body — particularly in delivery-delay disputes, which can often be resolved with a formal notice to the developer without a full lawsuit. On the other hand, if the other party has already started formal proceedings, or the dispute value is significant, we move straight to full representation to protect your position without delay.

Another example from our work in Jeddah

Other recurring scenarios include a commercial tenant needing a retail lease reviewed before signing to avoid unfavorable renewal or eviction terms, a partner in an import company facing a dispute over profit distribution, or a family needing to document a sale of an inherited property among heirs to avoid a future dispute. We help with all of these, from document review through to full representation if needed.

When you actually need a real estate lawyer in Jeddah

Clear signs that call for an immediate consultation: a residential unit delivered late against the agreed date, receiving an unjustified eviction notice, a vague clause in a property purchase contract before signing, or a dispute with a real estate broker over a commission that wasn't clearly agreed. Acting quickly on these signals saves you larger legal costs later.

Documents you may need before starting your file in Jeddah

For real estate cases, it helps to have a copy of the title deed or the lease contract in dispute, along with any correspondence with the developer, tenant, or real estate broker if available. For commercial import cases, having a copy of the supply contract and relevant customs invoices helps. None of these documents are needed for the initial consultation — describing your situation clearly is enough, and we only ask for official documents once you move to actually filing a claim or full representation before the relevant authority.

Expected timeline for a Jeddah file

In delivery-delay disputes, a formal notice to the developer is often enough to move the file within days. For cases that actually reach the Commercial Court, the timeline depends on the court's own schedule and the dispute's complexity, and we give you a clear estimate of that timeline right after assessing your file, rather than vague general promises.
