Our Afdal Mohami team in Riyadh provides licensed legal advice and representation for individuals and companies in the capital, from major commercial matters to family disputes, with full WhatsApp follow-up from first contact to case closure.

Riyadh isn't just the administrative capital — it's the country's commercial and financial center of gravity. The head offices of most major companies, banks, and investment funds sit here, alongside ministries and regulators such as the Ministry of Justice and the Capital Market Authority. That concentration shapes the kind of cases we see in Riyadh; alongside routine personal-status and labor matters, the city carries a heavier share of complex commercial disputes, corporate governance issues, and investment-related work.

Most requested services in Riyadh

Company formation tops the list of requests in the capital, given the concentration of founders and investors here, closely followed by demand for commercial debt collection between companies — a recurring dispute in a business environment the size of Riyadh's. Commercial arbitration is also increasingly popular as a faster alternative to traditional litigation for large contracts, particularly in construction and technology. On the family side, divorce and khula and custody and alimony remain among the most common files our team handles in the general courts here.

Our Riyadh work isn't limited to the private sector — with strong growth in banking and financial services and major infrastructure expansion, we also handle regulatory compliance files, financing agreements, and contractor disputes tied to large construction projects across the capital and its suburbs.

Courts and competent authorities

Riyadh has a full set of specialized courts that route cases by nature: the Commercial Court for disputes between companies, the Labor Court for employment and end-of-service matters, the General Court for personal status and civil cases, and the Execution Court for enforcing judgments. We track all of these electronically through the Ministry of Justice's Najiz platform, so any update on your file reaches you directly without repeated in-person visits.

Common scenarios we deal with in Riyadh include a dispute between co-founders over equity split when setting up a startup, a contractor chasing overdue payments from a major real estate developer, or an employee whose contract was terminated without notice and needs to claim end-of-service dues. Each situation gets its own plan of action, but the starting point is always the same: a quick WhatsApp consultation to understand the position before any formal step is taken.

Initial consultation vs. full representation

Not every case needs full court representation from day one. Many of our Riyadh clients start with a quick consultation aimed at understanding their legal position and assessing how strong their case is before deciding to pursue formal action. In other situations — particularly commercial disputes between companies — full representation is necessary from the start to protect the client's interests, especially if the other party has already begun formal proceedings. We explain this distinction transparently at first contact, without pushing you toward a costlier option you don't actually need.

Another example from our work in Riyadh

Cases we regularly handle in the capital include a tech startup needing an investment contract reviewed before signing to ensure no clause weakens the founders' position later, a foreign investor needing to understand licensing requirements before setting up a business in the capital, or an executive facing a non-compete clause after leaving a job who needs to know whether it's legally enforceable. All these situations share one thing in common: the need for clear legal input before making a decision that's hard to reverse later.

When you actually need to retain a lawyer in Riyadh

Not every legal situation calls for immediately retaining a lawyer, but there are clear signs that it does: receiving an official notice from a government body or court, receiving a financial claim from another party, wanting a high-value contract reviewed before signing, or facing a family dispute that's escalating. In these situations specifically, a quick consultation helps you avoid mistakes that are hard to correct once it's too late — especially with official documents.

Documents you may need before starting your file in Riyadh

Required documents vary by case type, but there are common basics that speed up getting started on your file. For commercial cases, it helps to have a copy of the commercial registration and the disputed contract if one exists, along with any prior correspondence with the other party. For labor cases, having a copy of the employment contract and the termination letter, or proof of your start date, helps. For personal status cases, it's enough at first to simply explain the situation clearly — we'll guide you afterward toward the official documents your specific case path requires. You don't need any of these documents at the initial consultation stage; describing the situation is enough, and we only ask for documents once you actually move to representation or drafting.
